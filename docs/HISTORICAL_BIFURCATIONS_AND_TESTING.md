## 1. Epistemological Purpose & Dynamic Landscape

In Ether, history is neither an arbitrary sequence of unconstrained human choices nor a completely deterministic linear clock. Instead, civilizational trajectories unfold across a **multidimensional potential energy landscape** constrained by invariant physical, chemical, and thermodynamic laws (Tier 1).

Within this landscape, civilizational trajectories occasionally encounter **critical bifurcation thresholds** (tipping points) where the system's sensitivity to forcing surges ($\lambda_{\text{Lyapunov}} > 0$). At these junctures, an unforced deterministic model experiences a sharp divergence from the empirical historical record ($\Omega(t) \gg 0$).

### Formal Invariance Theorems Verified
1. **Identity Invariance**: In the absence of exogenous forcing ($\mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = 0$), identical random seeds $S_0$ produce bit-identical historical trajectories ($\Delta_{\text{IEEE-754}} = 0.0$).
2. **Metastability Potential Barrier**: Transition to a new civilizational attractor requires an activation energy impulse $\Delta E \ge E_{\text{barrier}}$. Below $E_{\text{barrier}}$, societal state returns exponentially to the original equilibrium well.
3. **Minimal Necessary Forcing ($\mu^*$)**: For every tipping point, there exists a unique scalar threshold $\mu^*$ such that $\forall \mu < \mu^*$, the system fails to transition, and $\forall \mu \ge \mu^*$, the transition occurs with probability $P = 1.0$.
